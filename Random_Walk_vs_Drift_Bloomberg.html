💬 Key Discussion Questions & Answers
1. Why can't we predict tomorrow's stock price from today's news? ▼

Answer: In competitive financial markets, all publicly available information is instantaneously incorporated into the current price. Therefore, only new, unpredictable information (εt) can change tomorrow's price. This is the core implication of the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH).

2. Why do stock markets still increase over decades despite daily noise? ▼

Answer: While daily market news is zero-mean random noise (εt), the long-term stock market possesses a small, constant Positive Drift (μ > 0). This drift represents real economic value creation: productivity, inflation, innovation, and corporate earnings expansion over time.

3. Can pure randomness produce a graph that looks like a trend? ▼

Answer: YES! Cumulative sum of random shocks creates "memory" in price levels (Unit Root). A series of consecutive positive shocks produces a visual "bull market" illusion, while negative shocks produce a "bear market" illusion — even when the underlying process has zero drift!

4. What creates the long-run upward movement in stock markets? ▼

Answer: Long-term compounding drift (μ). Over 10 to 30 years, cumulative μ dominates the random noise εt, driving index levels steadily upward.

📋 Instructor Teaching Notes & Facilitation Guide

Objective of this Interactive Slide

Help MBA students discover the concepts of Random Walk and Random Walk with Drift through live experimentation before introducing formal econometrics (Unit Roots & Stationarity).

Suggested Classroom Agenda (35–45 Mins)

  • Phase 1: Experimentation (5 mins) — Click "Next Day" or "Simulate 30/100 Days" on Pure Random Walk. Let students watch the price chart evolve. Point out how "trends" naturally form.
  • Phase 2: Auto-Pause & Question 1 (10 mins) — At Day 20, the simulation auto-pauses. Ask students: "Do you believe tomorrow's movement is predictable?" Allow discussion before clicking "Reveal Explanation".
  • Phase 3: Introduce Drift & Extended Simulation (10 mins) — Switch to Walk + Drift (μ = 0.10%). Run 200 or 300 days simulation to dramatically show how μ compounds over multi-hundred day horizons while pure noise fluctuates.
  • Phase 4: Real Asset Discussion & Theory (15 mins) — Discuss real-world assets (NIFTY 50 vs. Gold vs. Bitcoin). Connect to Unit Roots (β = 1) and why econometricians use log returns instead of price levels.

Common Student Misconceptions to Address

  • Misconception 1: "A strong upward trend means the stock is predictable."
    Correction: Show that pure random walk generates cumulative trends with zero predictability.
  • Misconception 2: "If news was positive today, tomorrow will be positive too."
    Correction: Tomorrow's news shock is independent of today's news.
  • Misconception 3: "Stock markets are pure random gambling."
    Correction: Real markets have positive structural drift (μ > 0) driving long-term wealth creation.
📚 Student Concept Guide: Random Walk & Drift

1. Pure Random Walk Model

In a Pure Random Walk, today's price level (Pt) equals yesterday's price level (Pt-1) plus an unpredictable random news shock (εt):

Pt = Pt-1 + εt    where E[εt] = 0

2. Random Walk with Drift Model

In real equity markets, prices experience daily random shocks plus a small, consistent positive drift (μ > 0) reflecting long-term economic expansion:

Pt = Pt-1 + μ + εt    where μ > 0

3. Why Analysts Use Returns Instead of Raw Prices

Because price levels (Pt) accumulate random shocks over time (Unit Root process), raw prices are non-stationary and can cause false regressions. Financial analysts calculate log daily returns (Δ ln Pt) to make data stationary for econometric forecasting!
